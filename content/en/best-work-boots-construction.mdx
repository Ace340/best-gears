## When IS it worth spending $200+?

If you do structural, demo, or heavy hauling EVERY day, a premium boot (Timberland PRO, KEEN, Wolverine) lasts 2–3 seasons where a budget one lasts one. The math changes when the abuse is constant. For starting out, general site work, or tight budgets: the boots above deliver.

## Steel vs composite toe: which do you need?

| | Steel toe | Composite toe |
|---|---|---|
| Protection | Maximum | Very good |
| Weight | Heavier | Lighter |
| Cold touch | Yes (conducts) | No |
| Metal detectors | Triggers them | Passes |

Falling-object risk (structural, demo): steel, no question. If you pass through detectors (airports, some plants) or work outdoors in winter: composite.

## FAQ

**How often should I replace budget boots?**
With daily use, expect 10–14 months of life. When the sole wears through or water starts getting in, it's past time.

**Are cheap boots actually safe?**
If they carry ASTM/CSA certification, yes — the steel toe passes the same tests as a premium boot's. The difference is durability and comfort, not safety.

**What size should I order?**
Measure your foot at the end of the day (it's more swollen) and check the product size guide. On the KELODON especially: measure before ordering.
